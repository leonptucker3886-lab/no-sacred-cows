SEO died and nobody held a funeral

Preconception

If you rank on Google for the right keywords, customers will find you. Ranking is the whole game.

For twenty years, that was true. You found keywords, you wrote pages, you climbed a blue-link list, you got clicks. In 2026 the list is gone for a huge share of searches. People ask an AI and get one answer — not ten options, one. Either the machine cites you or, as far as the customer knows, you do not exist.

SEO is not dying of neglect. It died of success: the industry optimized so hard that the answers became interchangeable sludge, and then a machine showed up that can generate infinite sludge for free. Search engines are now in the awkward position of ranking content that nobody needed a search engine to produce.

What actually happened to the clicks

The mechanics are simple. AI Overviews and chat assistants answer the question on the results page itself. The user's question gets resolved without a click. Informational queries — the bread and butter of the old SEO playbook — have collapsed hardest, because "what is X" is exactly what a language model is best at answering. What still gets clicked is what cannot be summarized: the tool, the booking page, the local operator, the thing that actually does the work.

Old game vs new game

The irony nobody in the industry will say plainly

The winners of the AI search era are the pages that were always worth reading. Clear answers, specific numbers, real experience, a human who takes a position. The losers are the people who played the old game well — the keyword-stuffers, the template-post publishers, the people who wrote for a crawler instead of a customer. The machines were built by reading the internet, and they learned to tell the difference.

The uncomfortable truth. If your content strategy can be summarized as "keywords with search volume," you are not doing marketing. You are doing arbitrage on a ranker's blind spot, and the ranker just got eyes. The people maddest about AI search are the people whose content was never meant to be read by a person.

Will the AI cite you? Run the checklist

Click every statement that is true of your site.

What to actually do

Write the answer a smart customer would ask for, in the first paragraph, then earn it with detail. Put real prices on pages. Keep the site fast and boringly crawlable — sitemaps, clean HTML, no JavaScript walls. Take positions, including unpopular ones, because agreeable mush is the first thing a summarizer discards. And get specific about your niche: the model looking for "how much does field software cost for a three-man crew" needs somebody on the internet who actually answered that with numbers. Be that somebody. It is far cheaper than it sounds, because almost nobody is doing it.

Bottom line

SEO didn't die because search stopped mattering. It died because the referee started reading the game instead of scoring it. The playbook that survives is the one that should have won all along: answer real questions with real substance and a real name on it. That was always the honest version of this job. Now it's the only version.
